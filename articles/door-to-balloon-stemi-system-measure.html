Door to Balloon STEMI System Measure

A Cardily data-literacy explainer on door to Balloon STEMI System Measure, focused on interpretation rather than invented precision.

2026-10-10 12 min read Intro Students

Key takeaways

  • Door-to-balloon (D2B) time measures minutes from hospital arrival to PCI in STEMI—system speed metric.
  • CMS reporting drove national improvement; floor effects now shift focus to first-medical-contact-to-device.
  • Denominator is STEMI patients undergoing primary PCI at participating hospitals—not all MI.
  • Shorter D2B improves outcomes when delays were long; marginal gains may diminish at ultra-low times.
  • Rural transfer patterns create competing metrics (door-in-door-out).

What the numbers are trying to measure

D2B time captures reperfusion speed for ST-elevation myocardial infarction treated with percutaneous coronary intervention. National registries (ACTION, CMS quality programs) track median and percent under 90 minutes.

First door-to-balloon includes transfers—subintervals isolate referral delays.

AHA quality chapters cite D2B improvement as success story with remaining rural gaps.

How to interpret them without being misled

Hospitals excluding eligible patients from reporting would artificially improve metrics—audit numerator integrity.

Symptom onset to balloon remains patient-centered timeline D2B omits.

Fibrinolysis-first systems use different quality measures—do not compare to PCI D2B blindly.

Common statistical traps

Ranking hospitals on D2B without case volume or transfer adjustment.

Assuming every minute saved equals identical mortality benefit at all baselines.

Ignoring non-PCI STEMI patients in denominators when discussing system performance.

What the data imply for learning and policy literacy

Regional STEMI systems coordinate EMS bypass—statistics reflect coordination, not single departments.

Equity: Black and rural patients face longer total ischemic times—D2B alone undercounts delay before arrival.

Pair D2B trends with OHCA and mortality for balanced quality view.

Responsible takeaways

Report median and 90th percentile, not only percent under threshold.

Include transfer metrics when present.

Not hospital shopping advice for acute symptoms—call emergency services.

Putting it together

Door-to-balloon STEMI measure literacy shows how policy metrics reshape cardiovascular care delivery statistics.
