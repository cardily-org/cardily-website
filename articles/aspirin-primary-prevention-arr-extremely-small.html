Aspirin Primary Prevention ARR Extremely Small

A Cardily data-literacy explainer on aspirin Primary Prevention ARR Extremely Small, focused on interpretation rather than invented precision.

2026-10-10 11 min read Intermediate Both

Key takeaways

  • Recent primary prevention aspirin trials in moderate-risk modern cohorts showed small absolute benefits offset by bleeding.
  • ARR for MACE in primary prevention is often a fraction of a percent over years—RR reductions can still look “significant.”
  • Age and bleeding risk dominate net benefit; population averages mislead individuals.
  • Declining smoking and better BP control lowered control-arm event rates vs older aspirin trials.
  • Guideline shifts reflect ARR/NNH balance, not aspirin “stopping working.”

Metric definition

Primary prevention aspirin trials count first MACE vs major bleeding over multi-year follow-up. Absolute risk reduction for MACE subtracts event rates; absolute risk increase for bleeding adds harm side.

Net clinical benefit metrics combine weights—transparent teaching should show unweighted absolutes first.

AHA updates cite evolving aspirin use prevalence from NHANES and surveys as behavior, not trial efficacy.

Where the data usually come from

ASPREE, ARRIVE, and ASCEND anchor modern ARR teaching with diverse populations (older, diabetic).

Older meta-analyses pooled eras with higher baseline risk—inflated apparent ARR if applied today.

CDC WONDER shows declining ischemic heart disease mortality trends affecting baseline risk context.

How trends can fool you

Applying 1990s ARR to 2020s lower-risk cohorts overstates benefit.

Rising GI bleeding detection from PPI patterns changes harm numerators.

Self-reported aspirin use in BRFSS mixes primary and secondary prevention unless carefully filtered.

Equity and subgroup reading skills

Bleeding risk varies with access to gastroenterology and H. pylori prevalence—harm ARR not uniform.

Underserved communities may have used aspirin for primary prevention without shared decision support—deprescribing equity matters.

Avoid ageist blanket statements; frame with individualized risk tools clinicians use.

Questions to ask of any chart

Primary vs secondary prevention population?

MACE and bleeding absolutes over identical horizon?

Which baseline risk era?

Putting it together

Aspirin primary prevention ARR literacy is the canonical lesson that statistical significance ≠ clinical importance at population scale.

Students should memorize the habit: locate control-arm rate before applauding RR.

Not aspirin advice—personal decisions require clinical assessment.
