Number Needed to Treat and Harm Pairing

A Cardily data-literacy explainer on number Needed to Treat and Harm Pairing, focused on interpretation rather than invented precision.

2026-10-10 12 min read Intro Students

Key takeaways

  • NNT is 1/ARR when ARR is expressed as a decimal over a defined period-horizon mismatch invalidates pairing.
  • NNH uses absolute risk increase of harm; compare on same time scale as NNT.
  • Round NNT up for public communication conventions (treat 20.2 as 21) and state uncertainty from trials.
  • Low baseline risk inflates NNT even when relative benefit is real.
  • Pairing NNT and NNH prevents one-sided pharma or anti-pharma narratives.

What the numbers are trying to measure

Number needed to treat estimates how many people need an intervention for one additional benefit compared with control over the trial’s follow-up. It derives directly from absolute risk reduction, not relative risk reduction alone.

Number needed to harm is the reciprocal of absolute risk increase for a specified adverse outcome-myopathy, bleeding, hypotension-again tied to duration.

Confidence intervals for NNT are wide when event rates are low; point NNT alone overstates precision.

How to interpret them without being misled

A statin NNT of 50 over five years for MACE prevention differs from an SGLT2 NNT for HF hospitalization over two years-they are not interchangeable rankings of “better drug.”

When trials report composite endpoints, NNT prevents one composite event, which may not equal preventing one death.

Express paired NNT/NNH to the same population: trial entrants, not general population averages.

Common statistical traps

Calculating NNT from relative risk reduction without control event rate-invalid.

Using median follow-up when events accrue non-uniformly without person-time alignment.

Ignoring competing mortality that removes patients from harm denominators.

What the data imply for learning and policy literacy

Shared decision aids use NNT frames; policy models multiply prevented events by eligible population from NHANES prevalence estimates.

AHA summaries of therapeutic advances sometimes cite illustrative NNTs-trace back to source trials.

Equity: if only affluent patients receive therapy, realized population NNT is worse than trial NNT.

Responsible takeaways

Always state event type, horizon, and confidence interval when citing NNT/NNH.

Teach reciprocals as pedagogical tools, not mystical integers.

Not treatment recommendations.

Putting it together

NNT and NNH pairing is core Cardily statistics ethics: absolute, paired, time-stamped.

Links forward to aspirin, GLP-1, SGLT2, and ARNI articles with consistent framing.

Educational literacy for students and public readers.
