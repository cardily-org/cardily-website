Confounding Indication Bias Explained

An educational guide to confounding Indication Bias Explained, focused on research literacy rather than hype.

2026-10-10 13 min read Intro Students

Key takeaways

  • Confounding by indication occurs when sicker patients receive aggressive therapy—observational comparisons favor less-treated groups or exaggerate treated groups depending on trajectory bias.
  • Time-varying treatments in HF (initiate after decompensation) create immortal time and channeling biases.
  • Propensity scores and marginal structural models attempt adjustment but cannot fix unmeasured confounding.
  • Relative benefits in registries often shrink toward null vs RCTs for the same drug class.
  • Translation: observational studies generate hypotheses; RCTs anchor causal claims for guidelines.

Big idea in one paragraph

Clinicians prescribe based on prognosis—exactly when confounding by indication thrives.

Cardiology registries for PCI, ICDs, and new drugs illustrate treatment selection tied to severity.

Research literacy distinguishes association from causation in real-world data.

Not arguing against registries—arguing for correct inference standards.

Key methods learners should recognize

Cohort studies adjust for measured covariates (EF, creatinine, comorbidity scores).

Instrumental variable analyses occasionally use regional practice variation—assumptions debatable.

Target trial emulation frameworks specify hypothetical RCT protocol applied to observational data.

Negative control outcomes test residual confounding signals.

Landmark findings and honest limitations

SGLT2 initiators after hospitalization may appear sicker at baseline—initiation strategies bias early observational comparisons.

Relative mortality lower in treated arm may reflect survivor selection—inspect new-user designs.

Absolute standardized mortality differences after weighting still uncertain if unmeasured severity differs.

Compare observational HR magnitudes to trial HRs—large divergence hints bias.

Translation barriers

ACC/AHA recommendations prioritize RCTs; registries inform safety and gaps.

Clinic decisions use trials; registries monitor rare harms post-market.

EHR-based studies increasingly clinic-linked—transparency on algorithms needed.

Causal ML methods emerging—not default standard.

Putting it together

Indication bias is the central reason observational hero narratives require humility.

Absolute rates and design matter more than fancy statistics labels.
