Immortal Time Bias Classic Trap

A Cardily data-literacy explainer on immortal Time Bias Classic Trap, focused on interpretation rather than invented precision.

2026-10-10 11 min read Intro Both

Key takeaways

  • Immortal time bias misclassifies early follow-up when exposure is defined after cohort entry but analysis credits protection during that window.
  • Common in observational drug studies: users must survive or remain eligible to become “users.”
  • Bias often exaggerates treatment benefit or harm depending on design.
  • Time-varying Cox models and clone-censor-weight approaches reduce bias when applied correctly.
  • Cardiology registry studies of devices and drugs require immortal-time literacy.

Metric definition

Immortal time refers to periods where, by design, an outcome of interest (often death) cannot occur under the analytic rules—yet person-time is assigned to a exposure group incorrectly.

Classic example: comparing initiators vs never-users without aligning time zero at treatment eligibility.

Bias magnitude grows when initiation delay is long—common for specialty referrals and insurance authorization.

Where the data usually come from

Claims and EHR observational studies of statins, beta-blockers post-MI, or ICD implants frequently debate immortal time correction.

Randomized trials avoid this by randomizing at enrollment with intention-to-treat analysis.

NHANES cross-sectional studies face different biases; immortal time appears in longitudinal linkage studies.

How trends can fool you

Apparent survival advantage for treated groups may reflect months of guaranteed survival before treatment start counted as treated time.

Quality metrics rewarding early prescription can worsen bias if analysts rush naive comparisons.

Pregnancy exposure windows create related time-related misclassification—cousin bias family.

Equity and subgroup reading skills

Delayed treatment initiation in underserved groups can artificially worsen untreated arm outcomes in naive studies—misleading equity conclusions.

Read methods sections for time-zero alignment, not only hazard ratios in abstracts.

Advocacy using flawed observational stats can misallocate interventions.

Questions to ask of any chart

When does follow-up clock start for each arm?

Time-varying exposure modeling?

Landmark analysis at fixed time post-diagnosis?

Putting it together

Immortal time bias is a classic trap Cardily readers use to filter observational cardiology headlines.

Pair with likelihood ratios and trial ARR articles to contrast study designs.

Educational epidemiology—not study conduct advice.
