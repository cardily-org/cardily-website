Understanding Pulmonary Hypertension Groups: A Clear Educational Guide

An evidence-aware educational overview of pulmonary hypertension groups, written for students and curious readers seeking clarity without personal medical advice.

2026-10-10 12 min read Advanced Both

Key takeaways

  • WHO pulmonary hypertension groups classify hemodynamic etiologies: PAH (1), left heart disease (2), lung disease/hypoxia (3), CTEPH (4), unclear/multifactorial (5).
  • Group determines therapy—PAH drugs are not for all pulmonary hypertension.
  • Right heart catheterization with wedge pressure and PVR defines groups formally.
  • Echo estimates pulmonary pressures but cannot alone assign group.
  • Classification literacy prevents treatment errors and anchors research enrollment.

Why this diagnosis matters

Pulmonary hypertension is elevated mean pulmonary artery pressure on hemodynamic testing. The WHO grouping separates mechanisms so clinicians choose appropriate pathways.

Group 1 PAH is pre-capillary with elevated PVR; Group 2 reflects post-capillary elevation from left heart failure or valvular disease with wedge pressure context.

Group 3 arises from chronic lung disease and hypoxia; Group 4 from chronic thromboembolic obstruction amenable sometimes to surgery.

Misconception: one ‘pulmonary hypertension pill’ treats all groups.

Mechanisms and natural history

Left heart disease elevates pulmonary venous pressure, eventually remodeling arterioles secondarily.

Hypoxic vasoconstriction in COPD or sleep apnea chronically raises pressures.

Unresolved emboli organize into chronic obstructions raising PVR in CTEPH.

Group 5 captures multifactorial or unclear cases like hematologic disorders.

Differential thinking

Differentiate PAH from pulmonary venous hypertension due to mitral stenosis using wedge and waveforms.

Screen CTEPH with V/Q scan in guideline teaching when PH unexplained.

Connect systemic sclerosis to PAH subset in rheumatology-cardiology overlap.

Student drill: Assign example patients to groups before seeing cath data, then revise after.

Treatment categories at a high level

Group 1 therapies: endothelin antagonists, PDE5 inhibitors, prostacyclins, sGC stimulators in combinations.

Group 2: treat left heart failure, valves, ischemia—not PAH vasodilators alone.

Group 3: oxygen, lung disease management; PAH drugs generally avoided.

Group 4: pulmonary thromboendarterectomy or balloon angioplasty in expert centers when operable.

How to keep learning responsibly

Memorize group numbers with anchor examples, not exhaustive lists initially.

Follow world symposium updates when classifications refine subgroups.

Discuss access to PH specialty centers regionally.

Study exercise limitation as shared symptom across groups with different fixes.

Putting it together

Pulmonary hypertension groups organize a heterogeneous pressure elevation syndrome by mechanism.

Catheter hemodynamics plus clinical context assign group; echo alone is insufficient for therapy class choice.

Cardily builds classification literacy; PH centers manage vasoactive regimens and operability assessments.
